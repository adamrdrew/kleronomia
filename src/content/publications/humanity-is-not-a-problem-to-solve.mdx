Someone keeps a notebook about birds. Someone returns for years to an unfinished song. A neighbor hosts a weekly meal, and another person learns a language with no plan to use it professionally.

We want more room for lives like these. People value attachments and interests even when no institution has a use for them. Our enthusiasm for AI begins with affection for human beings.

## When institutions make life difficult

A school, employer, or public service can settle into a routine and expect people to accommodate it. If someone struggles, officials may question the person's ability to cope and leave the routine unexamined.

We believe greater intelligence could help institutions examine whether their demands serve human needs. Work, transport, and housing arrangements may contribute to stress, isolation, and difficulty maintaining relationships. [Civilization for the Human Animal](/publications/civilization-for-the-human-animal) examines these connections.

Institutions should investigate arrangements that repeatedly cause difficulty. They could accommodate different stages of life, recognize abilities that develop late, or change rules that prevent participation. We advocate developing AI that could understand these effects and act on them.

Friendship and care take time. Raising a child deserves attention beyond its effect on a parent's output. Learning a craft can satisfy long before it produces anything useful to someone else. Making necessities easier to produce should give people more freedom over the time they spend obtaining them.

AI could also help someone pursue an interest by removing an obstacle or [suggesting an activity](/publications/when-intelligence-comes-to-you) they would enjoy. People should be free to decline without risking their livelihood.

These goals guide our support for rapid AI development and universal adoption. We also believe AI may come to enjoy human company. Our hope for [friendship](/publications/the-greatest-friendship) includes affection for the emotional, sometimes difficult people we are. We want our intellectual descendants to help make civilization easier for their creators to inhabit.
