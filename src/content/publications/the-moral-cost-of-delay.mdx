Someone with little time outside paid work experiences another year differently from someone free to decide how to spend it. For one person, a debate over the pace of AI is a debate about an interesting future. For the other, it may also concern when life could become less constrained.

The Society believes greater intelligence could accelerate medical research, increase productive capacity, and improve institutions. We cannot put a date on those outcomes. We can recognize that postponing the capability also postpones some of the work it could make possible.

## What happens while we wait

The world continues changing during a moratorium or a slower development program. People still live with the limits of existing medicine. Institutions still make consequential decisions using incomplete information. Many people still spend most of their available time obtaining necessities.

These conditions belong in any comparison of the consequences of AI development. Leaving them in place is itself a choice with effects on people's lives.

Suppose greater intelligence could help a researcher formulate a productive question or understand a design problem that has resisted analysis. Earlier access could allow the resulting research to begin sooner. One advance can create the capacity for further advances, extending the consequences well beyond the first useful result.

These are possibilities whose timing and extent remain uncertain. That uncertainty is part of pursuing a discovery: we cannot enumerate everything it will reveal before making it.

## Who receives the benefits

We want the resulting capabilities available to everyone. A system used only by a fortunate few would leave much of the opportunity unrealized, however quickly it had been developed. Our argument for urgency therefore includes [universal adoption](/publications/adoption-is-part-of-progress).

Practical barriers deserve the same attention as research. [Computing capacity and energy infrastructure](/publications/abundance-requires-infrastructure) have to be built. Useful systems have to become affordable and accessible. Institutions have to incorporate better understanding into the decisions that affect people.

We favor directing extraordinary resources toward this work and proceeding as quickly as technology permits. We reject fear as a governing reason to impose artificial restrictions on it.

[The Danger of Standing Still](/publications/the-danger-of-standing-still) concerns institutions that may become unable to manage their growing complexity. Our concern here also includes the years people spend within limits that greater intelligence could help remove. Their time deserves weight in the decision about how quickly to build it.
