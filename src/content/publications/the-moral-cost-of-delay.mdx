A delay can look like the absence of a decision. Leave the future for later. Keep the present arrangements. Wait until the argument feels easier to settle.

But waiting is itself a decision about the lives that unfold in the meantime.

The Society believes that artificial intelligence offers extraordinary possibilities for scientific discovery, material abundance, medical progress, creativity, and the transformation of institutions. If we take those possibilities seriously, postponing their development cannot be treated as morally weightless.

## The present is not an empty baseline

Discussion of a transformative technology often compares an uncertain future with an imagined present in which nothing consequential changes. Actual life does not pause while the discussion continues.

People continue living under the limits of existing knowledge and productive capacity. Institutions continue making decisions with incomplete information. Human beings continue selling much of their time to meet the conditions of survival. Opportunities that might have existed with greater understanding remain unavailable.

The Society does not claim to know an exact timetable for ending these conditions. We reject the idea that the lack of such a timetable makes the value of progress disappear. A discovery does not become unimportant because its arrival cannot be scheduled in advance.

## Opportunity belongs in the accounting

To consider the consequences of acceleration is also to consider the consequences of withholding it. Both choices shape the future. Neither can be understood by examining only one side of the comparison.

Suppose greater intelligence could help researchers formulate a productive question, improve a complex design, or understand a system that currently resists human analysis. Delaying that capability would postpone not just the capability itself but some of the work that might follow from it.

These are possibilities, not documented results of an unspecified future system. Their significance is that a technological undertaking can have value extending beyond its immediate product. An advance in intelligence may become an advance in the capacity to make other advances.

This is why the Society describes acceleration as a civilizational priority rather than a preference for faster product releases.

## The uneven burden of standing still

A comfortable person may experience delay as a longer debate about the future. Someone constrained by economic necessity may experience it as another stretch of a life with very little room for anything else.

The Society believes the benefits of greater intelligence should reach every human being. That conviction gives urgency to both development and [universal adoption](/publications/adoption-is-part-of-progress). A future that is indefinitely postponed, or available only to a narrow group, does not fulfill the opportunity we are advocating.

We should therefore ask concrete questions about the resources, infrastructure, and institutions that could make more intelligence available. We should direct extraordinary energy toward the undertaking. We should not treat the existing distribution of possibility as a morally privileged arrangement simply because it is familiar.

## Urgency can build

Acceleration is sometimes imagined as a refusal to do difficult practical work. Our position is the opposite. If intelligence matters this much, the practical work matters enormously.

[Infrastructure must be built](/publications/abundance-requires-infrastructure). Capabilities must be developed. Useful systems must be adopted. Institutions must learn to use greater understanding in the decisions they make. People must be able to encounter the benefits in their own lives.

The Society favors doing this work as rapidly as technology and resources permit. We reject artificial restrictions imposed by fear as the governing principle of humanity's most consequential technological undertaking.

## A responsibility to possibility

We do not know every discovery a more intelligent civilization could make. That is part of the point of creating one.

The inability to enumerate the future is not a reason to place no value on it. Human beings have always pursued understanding in circumstances where the full consequences of understanding were unavailable in advance.

We believe the coming expansion of intelligence deserves that same confidence, at extraordinary scale. [The danger of standing still](/publications/the-danger-of-standing-still) is not only that present institutions may become less able to cope with their own complexity. It is also that lives could remain unnecessarily confined by limits we have an opportunity to transcend.

**The future is not costless to postpone.**
