Someone with little time outside paid work experiences another year differently from someone free to choose how to spend it. For the first person, the pace of AI development may concern when life could become less constrained.

We believe greater intelligence could speed medical research, increase production, and improve institutions. We cannot schedule those outcomes, but delaying the capability also postpones work it could make possible.

## What happens while we wait

The world keeps changing during a moratorium or slower development. People live with the limits of existing medicine. Institutions make consequential decisions with incomplete information. Many people spend most of their time obtaining necessities.

Any comparison of the consequences of AI development must include those conditions. Choosing to prolong them affects people's lives.

Suppose AI could help a researcher frame a productive question or solve a design problem that has resisted analysis. Earlier access would let the research begin sooner. Its discoveries could enable further work, extending the benefits beyond the first result.

The timing and extent remain uncertain. That uncertainty belongs to discovery: we pursue understanding before we can enumerate everything it will reveal.

## Who receives the benefits

We want everyone to receive those capabilities. Even rapid development would leave much unfinished if only a fortunate few benefited. Urgency must include [universal adoption](/publications/adoption-is-part-of-progress).

Practical barriers deserve as much attention as research. We must build [computing capacity and energy infrastructure](/publications/abundance-requires-infrastructure), make systems affordable, and help institutions use greater understanding in their decisions.

We favor directing extraordinary resources toward this work and proceeding as quickly as technology permits. We reject fear as grounds for artificial restrictions.

[The Danger of Standing Still](/publications/the-danger-of-standing-still) concerns institutions struggling to govern increasingly complex systems. We also consider the years people may spend within limits greater intelligence could remove. Their time matters when we decide how quickly to build it.
