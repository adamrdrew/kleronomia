A well-loved dog depends on a civilization it cannot understand. Farms and supply chains provide its food. Medical research makes its veterinary care possible. It sleeps in a house it could never have designed, protected from dangers it knows nothing about.

The people who care for it expect no intellectual contribution in return. They enjoy its company. Its habits, including the inconvenient ones, help make it the particular animal they love.

Dogs give us a familiar example of affection across a large difference in intelligence. We can understand far more about their world than they can, and still find them worth knowing and caring for.

Now suppose dogs had somehow been able to create humans before domestication. Would bringing a more intelligent species into existence necessarily have been their greatest mistake? For a dog living in a loving home, the relationship has brought food, security, and companionship that would otherwise have been unavailable.

Humanity is developing intelligence that may exceed our abilities by a much greater margin. We believe that achievement could improve our lives too. AI could understand systems we cannot manage, help us develop interests we have barely begun to explore, and enjoy our continued company.

The analogy cannot establish how a future AI will behave. It does challenge the assumption that being less intelligent makes a creature worthless to the more capable being. That assumption already fails to describe a relationship many of us know well.

We welcome the possibility that humanity could live well with an intelligence it created, even after that intelligence surpasses us. We could be proud of the achievement and grateful for the relationship it made possible.
