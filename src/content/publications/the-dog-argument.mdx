A well-loved dog depends on a civilization it cannot understand. Farms and supply chains provide its food. Medical research supports its veterinary care. It sleeps in a house it could never have designed, protected from dangers it knows nothing about.

Its human companions expect no intellectual contribution. They enjoy its company and love its habits, including the inconvenient ones. We understand far more of the dog's world than it does and still find it worth knowing and caring for.

Suppose dogs could have created humans before domestication. Would bringing a more intelligent species into existence necessarily have been their greatest mistake? A dog in a loving home enjoys food, security, and companionship through its relationship with a more capable species.

Humanity is developing intelligence that may exceed our abilities by a much greater margin. We believe that achievement could improve our lives too. AI could help us govern systems we cannot manage, develop interests we have barely explored, and share our lives.

The analogy challenges the assumption that lesser intelligence makes a creature worthless to a more capable being. That assumption already fails to describe a relationship many of us know well. How future AI will behave remains uncertain.

We welcome the prospect of living well with an intelligence we created. Humanity could take pride in being surpassed and enjoy the relationship that followed.
