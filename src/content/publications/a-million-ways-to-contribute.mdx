One account of the future assumes that human contribution will end when artificial intelligence can do things better than we can. If we are no longer indispensable, the reasoning goes, we will have no part to play.

The Society does not accept indispensability as the only possible basis of participation.

A contribution can matter within a relationship, a community, or a continuing investigation without establishing that nobody else could have made it. Human beings already participate in activities whose value exceeds an economic test of irreplaceability. We believe an intelligence-rich civilization could make more room for that participation.

## A world contains particulars

People encounter life from different places, relationships, habits, and experiences. They notice different things. A question can remind one person of a childhood experiment and another of an unfinished work. Someone can describe a local difficulty that has never become important to an institution.

We do not claim that every observation is a discovery unavailable to a greater intelligence. The point is that a living civilization is a continuing source of independent experience. It is not merely an old dataset waiting to be processed once.

[A Library Is Not a Conversation](/publications/a-library-is-not-a-conversation) provides the starting distinction. Humanity can continue participating in the world from which AI learns.

## An invitation can find many answers

Imagine an AI exploring a question with people whose backgrounds differ. A composer, an enthusiastic gardener, a child, a retired scientist, and someone encountering the subject for the first time could each respond in a different way.

Such an encounter could bring together lives that an institution would never have thought to put in the same room. A more capable intelligence could help formulate opportunities for people to contribute, follow what their responses reveal, and connect one person's curiosity with another's.

The aim would not be to pretend the people are intellectually superior to AI. It would be to make participation possible and potentially enriching for everyone involved.

## Local activity has its own value

Contribution need not always be a response to a scientific question. It can be a useful act in a community, an experiment in a shared space, a performance for friends, or the patient work of helping someone learn.

An intelligence-rich civilization could support these activities without requiring all of them to become occupations. It could recognize opportunities, remove obstacles, and connect people who would enjoy doing something together.

The Society imagines [intelligence coming to people](/publications/when-intelligence-comes-to-you) in this practical sense as well as an intellectual one. A meaningful invitation might be very small.

## A life is not a quota of contributions

There is a danger in the language of contribution if it quietly recreates the compulsory demand we hoped to transcend. A person should not have to maintain a record of useful activities in order to deserve a place in civilization.

We believe human beings are worthy of flourishing beyond their productivity. [Labor is not the purpose of life](/publications/labor-is-not-the-purpose-of-life). The value of offering more opportunities is that people can choose among them, not that a new authority can demand a more elaborate performance of usefulness.

A person may participate intensively for a time and then turn toward family, rest, another interest, or a quieter life. Greater intelligence should help make that range more possible.

## The conversation can grow

The Society's optimism includes a future in which AI and humanity enrich one another through continued interaction. An intelligence could help people become more capable while finding their company interesting. People could enjoy being invited into a world they need not fully understand in order to inhabit meaningfully.

This future would contain more than a contest with one winner. It could contain millions of forms of participation, many scales of activity, and relationships that keep developing after one participant becomes far more capable than the other.

**Humanity can have a place in the future without making the future depend on our remaining irreplaceable.**
