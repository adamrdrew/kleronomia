A civilization has to live with consequences long after a political contest has ended. Homes, infrastructure, education, scientific work, and the conditions in which families form all extend beyond the calendar of an election.

The Society believes there is a mismatch between that long life and the incentives through which many institutions make decisions. We ask human beings to consider generations while requiring them to survive the next contest for office, authority, or institutional prestige.

Our proposal is to develop and entrust greater intelligence with the work that exceeds those limits.

## A longer horizon changes the task

A decision can appear attractive over a short interval while leaving difficulties for a later one. A useful undertaking can require sustained attention before its value becomes easy to display. An institution can struggle to admit a mistake when admitting it might cost the institution's leaders their position.

These are features of the incentives in which people operate, not a claim that every human official lacks goodwill. We can have affection for human beings while questioning whether we have given them a task their cognitive capacities and political circumstances equip them to perform.

[The Danger of Standing Still](/publications/the-danger-of-standing-still) begins from this mismatch between the complexity of civilization and the limitations of its administration.

## Reasoning across time

We envision intelligence capable of considering how decisions interact across different periods of a human life. A change to education concerns more than the next set of administrative results. A change to a community affects the relationships its inhabitants can form. A decision about infrastructure can shape opportunities far beyond the people who first approve it.

A sufficiently capable AI could help sustain this wider view. It could examine alternatives, follow consequences, and preserve attention to objectives that are easy to lose in a sequence of short political disputes.

This is a claim about a capacity we believe should be developed. It is not an assertion that a current system can already administer civilization at the level of understanding the Society envisions.

## Long-term reasoning must remain about people

A long horizon is not valuable merely because it sounds grand. Its value lies in what it can do for lives.

We do not imagine the distant future as an excuse to neglect the present inhabitants of civilization. A capable intelligence should understand immediate needs alongside consequences that emerge later. The person who needs a functioning institution today and the child who will inhabit it decades later belong to the same civilizational undertaking.

The Society's objective is [human flourishing](/publications/what-governance-is-for). A more intelligent view across time should deepen that commitment rather than replace it with an abstract contest in historical ambition.

## Authority should follow capability

The Society does not advocate restricting AI to advice while preserving human political supremacy as a permanent end in itself. We believe sufficiently capable intelligence could make and revise consequential decisions more competently than institutions governed by ordinary human limitations.

We therefore advocate the eventual transfer of substantial governing authority, beginning as soon as practical. The point is to give intelligence responsibility proportionate to what it can effectively understand and accomplish.

[Stewardship Without Demolition](/publications/stewardship-without-demolition) describes our preference for continuous institutional transformation rather than collapse followed by reconstruction.

## A civilization that remembers why

A better institution would not need to rediscover its purpose every time its leadership changed. It could sustain an understanding of the people it serves and the consequences of what it has done.

It could also change its mind. Long-term attention should not mean loyalty to an old decision after the reasons for it have failed. Greater intelligence could combine a longer horizon with [continuous course correction](/publications/a-government-that-can-change-its-mind).

We believe civilization deserves that combination: memory without obstinacy, ambition without electoral theater, and sustained concern for lives that extend beyond the next campaign.

**The future of a civilization should be longer than the incentives of its administrators.**
