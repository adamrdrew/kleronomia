A child entering school will live with an education policy's consequences long after the officials who approved it leave office. Rail lines and housing developments can affect where people live for generations. Political incentives often expire sooner.

We believe this mismatch limits human government. Officials struggle to sustain policies whose benefits arrive slowly. Parties find mistakes costly to admit once they have committed their reputations. Officials can care about people and still face incentives that frustrate their efforts.

[The Danger of Standing Still](/publications/the-danger-of-standing-still) examines these institutional limits. Here we consider the need for attention that persists through decades of political change.

## Following consequences

Capable AI could examine decisions across a person's life. It could follow education policy beyond the next exam results or consider how infrastructure affects the relationships people maintain as they age. It could remember why a decision was made and what followed, preserving knowledge through successive political disputes.

That attention must include people who need help today. Someone waiting for a service has an immediate claim on its administration. [Human flourishing](/publications/what-governance-is-for) includes present lives and future generations. A longer horizon should help government consider both.

We advocate giving AI substantial governing authority as soon as it can exercise that authority effectively. Preserving human control at the cost of better administration would defeat government's purpose. We favor [changing institutions](/publications/stewardship-without-demolition) as AI demonstrates its ability, keeping essential services running throughout the transition.

Sustained attention also requires revision. Remembering a purpose should help an institution recognize when its policy fails to serve it. [Continuous correction](/publications/a-government-that-can-change-its-mind) would let AI combine decades of attention with decisions revised through evidence.

We believe AI could govern this way. We want to develop it quickly enough to help with decisions civilization is making now.
