A child entering school will live with the consequences of an education policy long after the officials who approved it leave office. A rail line or a housing development can affect where people live for generations. The decisions outlast the incentives under which they were made.

We believe that mismatch limits human government. A policy with benefits that arrive slowly can be difficult to sustain. A mistake can become costly to admit once a party has committed its reputation to it. Officials can care deeply about people and still operate within incentives that frustrate that care.

[The Danger of Standing Still](/publications/the-danger-of-standing-still) describes our broader concern about the limits of institutional capacity. Here the limitation is time: civilization needs attention that can persist through decades of political change.

## Following consequences

A sufficiently capable AI could examine how decisions affect different periods of a person's life. It could follow an education policy beyond the next set of exam results, or consider how infrastructure affects the relationships people can maintain as they age.

It could also retain knowledge of why a decision was made and what subsequently happened. Successive disputes would no longer need to erase the institution's understanding of its own actions.

We want that attention to include people who need help today. Someone waiting for a functioning service has an immediate claim on its administration. [Human flourishing](/publications/what-governance-is-for) includes present lives alongside those that will unfold later. A longer horizon should improve our ability to consider both.

The Society supports eventual AI governance. We advocate transferring substantial authority to sufficiently capable AI, beginning as soon as practical. Permanent human control is not an objective we would preserve at the expense of better administration.

The transition would depend on demonstrated ability to understand and perform the relevant functions. We favor [continuous institutional transformation](/publications/stewardship-without-demolition) while the people relying on those functions continue to receive them.

A longer commitment also needs the ability to revise. Remembering a purpose must leave room to discover that a particular policy no longer serves it. [Continuous correction](/publications/a-government-that-can-change-its-mind) would allow intelligence to combine decades of attention with changes informed by evidence.

We believe AI could make that combination possible, and want to develop it quickly enough to address the decisions civilization is already making.
