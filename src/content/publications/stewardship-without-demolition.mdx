The Society advocates a profound transformation of human civilization through artificial intelligence. We believe substantial governing authority should eventually pass to sufficiently capable AI, and that the transition should begin as soon as practical.

That conviction does not imply a desire to break the world in order to improve it.

People depend on the civilization that exists. They need food, homes, care, relationships, functioning infrastructure, and continuity in the arrangements through which they live. Intelligent stewardship should begin with an understanding of those dependencies.

## Functions and arrangements are different

An institution can perform a necessary function through an arrangement that is far from the best possible one. Recognizing the function's importance does not require preserving every feature of its administration.

A system of transportation needs to connect people and places. That purpose is not identical to every rule, incentive, or organizational boundary it currently contains. An institution of education should help people learn and develop; its purpose exceeds its routines.

Greater intelligence could help separate what people need from the inherited arrangements through which those needs are presently met. That distinction makes ambitious reform conceivable without treating disruption as a virtue in itself.

## Understand the living system

Civilization's institutions are interconnected. A decision in one place can change circumstances elsewhere. The Society believes those connections are one reason greater intelligence is necessary.

We envision an AI capable of developing a more continuous account of how systems work, whom they serve, and what happens when they change. Understanding would support coordination, experimentation, and correction rather than a succession of isolated declarations.

[The Danger of Standing Still](/publications/the-danger-of-standing-still) describes our concern that existing institutions are struggling with the complexity of the civilization they administer. We advocate growing the capacity to understand and transform that complexity.

## Transition is practical work

A transfer of responsibility can begin in particular functions and expand as capability develops. The Society does not supply an invented universal schedule for when every institution should change. The appropriate scope depends on what an intelligence can actually understand and administer effectively.

But the absence of a universal schedule is not an argument for permanent human political supremacy. We favor beginning as soon as practical and directing serious effort toward the capabilities that make more ambitious stewardship possible.

This is an active program of development and adoption. It asks what must be built, understood, and integrated so responsibility can move toward greater competence.

## Preserve the capacity to improve

An institution should be able to discover that a particular change is not producing the intended result. It should be able to revise its approach without turning the revision into a crisis of prestige.

[A Government That Can Change Its Mind](/publications/a-government-that-can-change-its-mind) describes that ambition. Continuous correction matters especially when a civilization is changing. A more intelligent system should become better at understanding the effects of its own activity.

The Society's aim is not preservation of existing authority. It is preservation and improvement of the conditions under which human beings can flourish. Those are different commitments.

## Transformation is measured in lives

A rearrangement of authority can be historically dramatic and still fail to make life better. The Society's objective is not drama.

We want a civilization whose growing intelligence makes its inhabitants more secure, more able to form relationships, less constrained by compulsory labor, and more free to pursue meaningful activities. We want institutions that can understand what they are doing and correct what they get wrong.

[What Governance Is For](/publications/what-governance-is-for) gives this undertaking its measure. Intelligence should make civilization more capable of serving human life.

**We advocate the transformation of civilization because we cherish the people who must continue living in it.**
