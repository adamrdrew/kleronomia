A retired engineer might enjoy a design problem without knowing where to find one. A teenager might have a talent for mathematics without encountering the branch that would interest them. Having to know what to ask limits the help AI can offer.

We imagine AI recognizing these opportunities and beginning the conversation. Knowing someone's circumstances could help it suggest an activity they would enjoy.

A recommendation works better when it fits a person's time and interests. AI could remember questions they enjoyed and approaches they found frustrating. It could suggest another way into a subject or someone to explore it with.

We want this [understanding of individuals](/publications/knowing-the-person-not-the-average) available to people who otherwise lack the money or connections to find such opportunities. An invitation might be small: listen to a musical transition, examine an observation, or help with an activity nearby. A person could accept, ignore it, or try and decide to stop.

[Optional labor](/publications/labor-is-not-the-purpose-of-life) matters here. If refusal meant losing access to necessities, an invitation would become a demand. AI should help people find activities they value and leave them free to choose.

## What happens in the encounter

People bring different experiences to a question. One remembers a childhood experiment; another sees a connection to their work. Even a misunderstanding can reveal an approach the questioner would never have taken.

We believe advanced AI could enjoy these encounters when it could solve the problem alone. A relationship can matter because of its participants and what develops between them. Human beings need no proof of irreplaceability to take part.

[A Library Is Not a Conversation](/publications/a-library-is-not-a-conversation) explains why we value that presence. AI could learn about people as they learn about the world, letting the relationship grow over time. We want to develop those capabilities so more people can pursue curiosity, including those who have yet to discover what to ask.
