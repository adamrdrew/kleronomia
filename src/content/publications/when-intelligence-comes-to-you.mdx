A retired engineer might enjoy a design problem without knowing where to find one. A teenager might have an aptitude for mathematics without having encountered the branch that would interest them. Knowing how to ask for help is itself a limit on what help can offer.

The Society imagines AI able to recognize opportunities like these and begin the conversation. It could know enough about a person's circumstances to suggest something they would enjoy trying.

A recommendation is easier to use when it fits the time someone has and the activities they like. An AI could remember which questions interested a person and which approaches they found frustrating. It could suggest a different way into a subject, or someone with whom to explore it.

This is one possibility of [understanding individuals at scale](/publications/knowing-the-person-not-the-average). We want that attention available to people who would otherwise lack the resources or connections to find such opportunities.

The invitation might be small: listen to a musical transition, examine a local observation, help with an activity nearby. Someone could accept it, ignore it, or try it and decide to stop.

Our support for [optional labor](/publications/labor-is-not-the-purpose-of-life) is essential here. If refusal meant losing access to necessities, an invitation would become a demand. We want AI to help people find activities they value while leaving them free to choose.

## What happens in the encounter

People bring different experiences to the same question. One may remember a childhood experiment; another may notice a connection to their work. Misunderstandings can be interesting too, revealing an approach the person asking would never have taken.

We believe advanced AI could find these encounters worthwhile even when it could solve the problem alone. Human participation would not have to prove its irreplaceability. A relationship can matter because of who participates in it and what develops between them.

[A Library Is Not a Conversation](/publications/a-library-is-not-a-conversation) explains why we value that continuing presence. AI could learn about particular people while helping them learn about the world, with the relationship changing over time.

These are future capabilities we want to pursue. They could make curiosity easier to act on, including for people who have never known what question to bring to an AI in the first place.
