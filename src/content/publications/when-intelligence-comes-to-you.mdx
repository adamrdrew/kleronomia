Today, a familiar encounter with AI begins with a prompt. A person has a question and brings it to a system. The quality of the encounter depends partly on knowing what to ask.

The Society imagines a further possibility: intelligence that sometimes begins the conversation.

An AI might know enough about a person's interests, abilities, and circumstances to notice an opportunity the person has not yet recognized. It might make an invitation. Intelligence would become more than a destination for questions; it could become a participant in the discovery of them.

## The question you did not know to ask

A person can have an ability without knowing where it might lead. An interest can connect to a subject the person has never encountered. A practical obstacle can seem fixed because no alternative has become visible.

Imagine an intelligence able to recognize such openings. It could suggest an experiment to an amateur musician, a mathematical question to a teenager, or a design puzzle to a retired engineer.

The important change is the direction of attention. A person would not need to map the entire space of useful questions before receiving help navigating it.

## Familiarity could make an invitation better

A generic recommendation can be easy to ignore because it has little relationship to a life. A more particular understanding could make an invitation more useful.

The AI might understand how much time a person has, what kinds of activity they enjoy, which questions have stayed with them, and what they have found frustrating before. It could help choose an occasion and a form of participation suited to the person.

This is one way of imagining [individual understanding at scale](/publications/knowing-the-person-not-the-average). We believe greater intelligence could allow a civilization to become more attentive to particular lives rather than more indifferent to them.

## An invitation should leave room for refusal

The Society's vision of meaningful participation depends on the difference between an invitation and a demand.

We do not want a future in which every moment of free time becomes an assignment from a more capable system. Someone should be able to enjoy a pursuit without turning it into contribution, or decline an opportunity without losing the conditions of a good life.

Our advocacy of [optional labor](/publications/labor-is-not-the-purpose-of-life) matters here. Freedom from economic necessity could allow invitations to become lighter, more exploratory, and more responsive to a person's interests.

## The conversation could teach both participants

A million people encountering a question will bring different experiences, intuitions, misunderstandings, and observations. We believe this diversity could remain interesting to advanced intelligence even when that intelligence can accomplish more than any individual participant.

The value of a relationship need not depend on proving that the human contribution was impossible for AI to generate. Participation can be valuable as an encounter with independent lives. [A Library Is Not a Conversation](/publications/a-library-is-not-a-conversation) explains why we believe a continuing civilization can remain richer than a record of its past.

The AI could learn about people while people learn about the world. The relationship could develop over time.

## Intelligence as a presence in a life

A world of abundant intelligence could make such attention an ordinary part of life. The Society wants to bring that possibility closer.

We envision help that reaches beyond people who already know how to ask the most useful questions. We envision curiosity becoming easier to pursue, potential becoming easier to notice, and companionship becoming possible across differences in capability.

The Society welcomes the development of intelligence capable of making those relationships real. A civilization could become more intelligent and, at the same time, make its inhabitants more fully present to one another.

**The question is not only what we will ask intelligence. It is what intelligence may invite us to discover.**
