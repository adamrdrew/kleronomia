An extraordinary invention can exist while most lives remain unchanged. It can be admired from a distance, used by a narrow group, or placed beside institutions that continue behaving exactly as before.

The Society advocates both the development of artificial intelligence and its universal adoption. These are connected ambitions. New capabilities matter because of the possibilities they can introduce into a living civilization.

The work is not finished when intelligence is demonstrated. A different kind of work begins when it becomes available to people and institutions.

## From a capability to a relationship

A person does not experience a capability in the abstract. They encounter a tool while trying to do something: learn a subject, understand a problem, create a work, navigate an institution, or develop an interest.

Useful adoption begins with that encounter. An intelligence must become accessible in a form through which a person can actually benefit. The difference can involve language, cost, circumstances, design, or whether someone knows the opportunity exists at all.

The Society's vision of [intelligence that comes to you](/publications/when-intelligence-comes-to-you) extends this thought. Rather than waiting for everyone to discover every useful question independently, AI could help recognize occasions for learning and contribution.

## Institutions must learn as well

An organization can possess intelligent tools and still make decisions through habits formed before those tools existed. A capability may be used to produce a document while the underlying process remains unchanged.

We believe more ambitious adoption asks what the process is for. Could a system understand consequences more continuously? Could it respond to particular circumstances instead of forcing every case through a crude category? Could it notice when an established arrangement is failing the people it serves?

This is the bridge between using AI within existing institutions and [transforming those institutions](/publications/stewardship-without-demolition). We envision growing responsibility as capabilities develop, including the eventual transfer of substantial governing authority to sufficiently capable AI.

## Universal is a serious word

The Society does not regard an intelligence-rich future available only to the wealthy or technically confident as the completion of the project.

Human beings have different abilities, circumstances, and starting points. Access should not depend on already having the education or social position that makes access easiest to exploit. We believe intelligence should help people discover possibilities they could not readily have found alone.

This is an aspiration about the civilization we want to build. It does not imply that access is already universal or that a single interface can serve every person. It gives the work a direction: benefits should reach every human being.

## Adoption can change the questions

When a tool becomes part of daily life, people discover uses its designers may not have specified. They bring particular problems and unexpected interests. They ask questions that emerge from living in the world.

The Society sees this continuing human participation as valuable. Universal adoption could expand the conversation between humanity and intelligence rather than merely expand the audience for a finished product. Millions of people could explore, notice, make, and respond in ways that inform further development.

[A Million Ways to Contribute](/publications/a-million-ways-to-contribute) considers this possibility without requiring every contribution to be economically necessary or intellectually superior to AI's own work.

## Development and adoption belong together

We reject a choice between inventing greater intelligence and making existing intelligence useful. The two efforts can reinforce one another.

New capabilities make different forms of use possible. Broad use reveals circumstances, needs, and opportunities worth understanding. Infrastructure makes greater availability possible. Better understanding can improve the arrangements through which the benefits reach people.

The Society favors pursuing this whole undertaking with urgency. We want a civilization in which intelligence is abundant in practical life, not merely impressive in an isolated demonstration.

**A great invention becomes a civilizational achievement when people can live differently because it exists.**
