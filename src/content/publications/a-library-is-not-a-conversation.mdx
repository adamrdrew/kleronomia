---
title: "A Library Is Not a Conversation"
description: "What a living humanity could offer AI after it has learned from our recorded knowledge."

Imagine an AI with access to every book, scientific discovery, work of art, and recorded conversation. Then imagine that humanity has disappeared.

It would have an immense archive. There would be nobody left to ask a new question or hear its reply.

People keep encountering the world from circumstances that differ from one another's. A childhood experiment can lead to an adult interest. A misunderstanding can prompt an unexpected question. Someone hears a piece of music differently after falling in love or losing a friend. Those experiences continue for as long as people are alive.

We believe AI could remain interested in that activity after it surpasses human capabilities. It could know people over many years, notice an aptitude, or invite someone to consider a problem. A person could respond with an observation from their own life, then change through the encounter.

The value of such company would not have to depend on supplying a fact that AI could never infer. We already enjoy conversations with people whose answers we can often anticipate. Their presence and participation matter to us.

The Society imagines humanity continuing to contribute experiences and questions to the world from which AI learns. Our intellectual descendants could meet the people who are still extending the culture they inherited.

A complete record of the past would give them much to read. We hope they would also want someone to talk to.
