Two people can share an income, age, and administrative status yet need different help. One may have family nearby; the other may care for someone alone. A category reveals part of each life and leaves much unexplained.

Large institutions rely on simplification because human administrators cannot know everyone they serve in detail. We believe greater intelligence could overcome that limit.

## Responding to changed circumstances

An AI that knew someone over time might notice when an arrangement stopped fitting their life. It could recognize an obstacle or aptitude and suggest an opportunity a general process would miss.

[When Intelligence Comes to You](/publications/when-intelligence-comes-to-you) explores those personal possibilities. Institutions could use the same understanding to respond to individuals and learn when their rules needed revision. These abilities remain a goal for development; current AI has yet to replace administrative categories with such understanding.

## The circumstances around a person

Several systems can produce a difficulty that looks individual. An appointment may be easy to offer and impossible to attend because of transport or care responsibilities. Changing one rule may leave the obstacle in place.

AI could trace these connections, help with the immediate problem, and investigate whether others face it too. Understanding individuals more fully could guide changes to the arrangements affecting them.

That is part of our case for [adapting civilization to human needs](/publications/civilization-for-the-human-animal). A person's body, relationships, and surroundings all affect what would help them live well.

We want everyone to receive this attention. Institutions should work as hard to understand someone with little influence as someone who already knows how to make them respond. That would require capacity for sustained attention to millions of lives.

We believe AI could provide it. [Human flourishing](/publications/what-governance-is-for) gives us reason to develop that capacity: understanding a person should help an institution serve them better.
