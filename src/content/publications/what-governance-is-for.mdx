Schools should help people learn. Transport should connect them to the places they need to go. Government should help them live well. Institutions have value because of what they make possible.

We believe capable AI could serve people better than existing political institutions. Give it substantial governing responsibility as soon as it can do so.

## Institutions are means

An institution can acquire supporters committed to preserving it. They may defend its procedures and history even when these obstruct its purpose.

We ask whether a better arrangement is available. Familiarity alone cannot justify preserving administration that another intelligence could improve. A coalition's survival matters less than how its government serves people.

Human beings need security, health, affection, and social connection. We need opportunities to form relationships, raise children, and pursue interests. These needs belong in any account of institutional success. [Civilization for the Human Animal](/publications/civilization-for-the-human-animal) develops that argument.

## Looking at the life affected

An economy can preserve jobs by retaining work machines could do. A service can process applications faster and still fail someone who fits none of its categories. Administrative success can coexist with lives made needlessly difficult.

AI could help institutions understand those failures by examining individuals and the systems that shape their circumstances. [Knowing a person](/publications/knowing-the-person-not-the-average) could reveal what an average misses.

The abilities we envision exceed those of current AI. We want to develop intelligence that can coordinate institutions, follow consequences across generations, and revise decisions as it learns. Governing authority should follow its ability to perform those tasks.

The aim is a civilization where someone can raise a child, practice a craft, or enjoy a community for its own sake. [Optional labor](/publications/labor-is-not-the-purpose-of-life) would give people more time for such lives.

Our [manifesto](/manifesto#principle-7) calls for adapting civilization to humanity. We would judge governing intelligence by how well it serves the people who depend on it.
