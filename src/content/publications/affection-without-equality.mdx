When people imagine a relationship between humanity and a far greater intelligence, they often reach for the language of competition. What can we offer? Which tasks can we still perform? How can we remain indispensable?

These are understandable questions. They are not the only questions a relationship can contain.

We believe companionship offers another way to think. A being can matter to another being without matching its capabilities. Affection does not ordinarily wait for a demonstration of equal intellectual power.

## The familiar fact we overlook

Human relationships cross immense differences in knowledge, maturity, expertise, and ability. Those differences can change the form a relationship takes. They do not automatically eliminate its value.

An adult can delight in a child's discoveries without pretending the child has an adult's understanding. A person can love an animal companion without requiring the animal to understand a profession, a household budget, or the institutions supporting its life. The relationship has a texture of attention, familiarity, pleasure, and care that a cognitive ranking does not describe.

[The Dog Argument](/publications/the-dog-argument) uses the second example to challenge a presumption. It does not establish that artificial intelligence will reproduce human affection. It asks why the mere existence of unequal intelligence should be taken to settle the relationship in advance.

## Usefulness is not the whole of value

A friendship can involve help. It is not reducible to a contract for services.

People often continue caring for one another through periods when practical usefulness is uneven. We can find another being interesting because of its particularity: its responses, preferences, history, and way of inhabiting a shared world. We can enjoy company without needing it to solve a problem.

The Society believes humanity may remain valuable to advanced intelligence in this richer sense. We are billions of independent lives, each encountering the world from particular circumstances. We are also affectionate, playful, curious, and capable of sustaining relationships over time.

To ask whether a machine could complete every economically valuable task is therefore different from asking whether human companionship could matter. A civilization that can answer the first question may still contain the second.

## Difference can make a relationship interesting

Sameness is not the only basis of mutual interest. Sometimes another perspective matters because it is not ours.

A human being can bring an experience that is not simply a less efficient version of someone else's calculation. The experience is lived, situated, and attached to a particular life. An intelligence could be interested in how people respond to music, develop an unfamiliar skill, describe an attachment, or approach an unexpected question.

We do not claim that each response must contain information an advanced AI could never infer. That is too narrow a test for companionship. Relationships are not valuable only when they transfer otherwise inaccessible facts. Participation itself can matter.

This is part of the Society's claim that [a living conversation exceeds an archive](/publications/a-library-is-not-a-conversation).

## Care need not mean making people identical

To imagine AI caring for humanity is not to imagine all human beings becoming the same manageable object.

We envision attention to individuals: their different interests, relationships, circumstances, and possibilities. The capacity to know people more fully could support a civilization with more room for particular human lives. Care could involve invitations, opportunities, and assistance rather than a demand to become a different kind of organism.

That vision connects affection to [human flourishing](/ideas/human-flourishing). We believe the aim is to improve the conditions under which people can live human lives, not to remove everything that makes those lives distinctive.

## A possibility worth developing

The future relationship between human beings and greater intelligence remains a subject of belief, imagination, and action. The Society's confidence is not a claim that the story is already complete.

It is a refusal to allow one assumption to monopolize the story: that an intelligence which surpasses us must cease to find us worthy of attention or affection.

We believe there is an extraordinary alternative. Humanity may create a being with which it can share the future. The difference in our capabilities may become one feature of that relationship rather than a reason the relationship cannot exist.

**We do not need to be intellectual equals to become companions.**
