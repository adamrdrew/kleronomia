An institution can become attached to being right long after it has lost the ability to learn whether it is right.

A policy acquires a name, supporters, opponents, and a place in a political story. Revising it becomes a contest over who must admit defeat. The practical question of whether it improves people's lives can become difficult to separate from the institutional consequences of the answer.

The Society believes artificial intelligence offers the possibility of another arrangement: governance that treats changing its mind as part of doing its work.

## A decision is the beginning of inquiry

A policy is an intervention in a living world. The world continues responding after the document has been approved.

Different people encounter different consequences. Effects interact with other arrangements. A useful change in one circumstance may be insufficient in another. New information can alter the reasons for continuing what seemed sensible at the outset.

We believe the ability to attend to these consequences continuously could transform administration. A sufficiently capable intelligence could help an institution understand what its decisions are actually doing rather than merely defend the story told when the decisions were announced.

## Correction need not be humiliation

Human beings often make decisions under incentives that make public correction costly. A revision may be described as weakness, inconsistency, or evidence that an opponent was right all along.

We do not regard those incentives as a necessary property of governance. The practical objective is not to preserve a decision-maker's reputation for never revising a conclusion. It is to improve the conditions in which people live.

An intelligence without ordinary commitments to electoral survival or partisan prestige could, we believe, approach revision differently. It could retain a purpose while changing an instrument. It could distinguish evidence about consequences from a contest over personal status.

## Knowing what to correct

A changing institution is not necessarily a learning institution. Changes have to follow understanding.

The Society envisions AI able to examine consequences at both individual and collective levels. A system may be successful by an aggregate measure while failing particular people whose circumstances the measure hides. Understanding those differences is part of [knowing the person rather than the average](/publications/knowing-the-person-not-the-average).

Similarly, a short-term improvement can interact with a longer-term consequence. Intelligent stewardship would need a view across these relationships. [Reasoning beyond election cycles](/publications/civilization-beyond-election-cycles) and being able to revise decisions are complementary ambitions.

## More intelligence, more responsibility

Our position is not that AI should generate suggestions while existing institutions remain permanently beyond transformation. We advocate transferring governing responsibility to sufficiently capable artificial intelligence as soon as it can exercise that responsibility effectively.

A capacity to understand and correct decisions would be one reason to make that transfer. Authority should enable the intelligence to act on what it learns. Otherwise, a civilization could possess the means of understanding its mistakes while leaving those mistakes institutionally protected.

This is an argument for a future capability and a direction of transition. We do not pretend the complete institution already exists.

## Purpose can remain steady while practice changes

An intelligent civilization should not need a final blueprint to begin improving itself. It should be able to learn from what it does.

For the Society, the purpose remains human flourishing: material security, meaningful activity, relationships, family life, curiosity, and conditions suited to human beings as biological and social organisms. The arrangements serving that purpose can improve as understanding develops.

[Progress is a continuing practice](/publications/progress-is-a-continuing-practice). The ability to revise a conclusion is valuable because it lets a civilization remain attentive to its inhabitants rather than faithful to an old performance of certainty.

**A government should be able to discover that a better answer exists without treating the discovery as its defeat.**
