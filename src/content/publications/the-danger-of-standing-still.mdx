A government deciding where to build homes must consider transport, employment, and the services residents will need. The consequences extend beyond the department that approves the decision and the terms of the officials who make it.

Civilization depends on countless decisions like this. Financial networks, infrastructure, and scientific institutions support cooperation on a scale earlier generations could barely imagine. No individual fully understands their interactions.

The Kleronomia Society believes civilization's complexity is outgrowing our ability to govern it. Human decision-makers have limited information and incentives that compound the difficulty. Elections favor quick results. Institutions defend established interests. Parties commit themselves to proposals, then find it costly to admit failure.

We believe capable AI could govern better. It could examine connections across institutions, understand individuals, and follow consequences over decades. It could revise decisions without needing to win an election or defend a reputation.

We want to develop that intelligence and give it responsibility as soon as practical. People will depend on functioning institutions throughout the transition. The governing intelligence must understand those dependencies, test changes, and correct mistakes as services continue.

Slower development has consequences too. Existing institutions will keep making decisions, and the systems they govern will keep changing. We believe their growing inability to coordinate civilization presents a greater danger than the speculative catastrophes invoked to restrict AI.

We advocate unrestricted acceleration to bring greater intelligence to problems developing now.
