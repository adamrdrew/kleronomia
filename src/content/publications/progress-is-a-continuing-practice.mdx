A transport change can improve one neighborhood's connections and lengthen someone else's journey. An education policy can succeed overall and still fail particular students. Understanding an institution requires following what happens after it acts.

We advocate developing AI that can sustain this attention across more lives and longer periods than human administrators can manage. It could help institutions improve as their decisions' consequences emerge.

Institutions interact. A useful adjustment in one can cause problems elsewhere, sometimes years later. Greater intelligence could evaluate alternatives, examine the results, and decide what to try next. It could identify mistakes human administrators lack the information or incentive to acknowledge.

[A Government That Can Change Its Mind](/publications/a-government-that-can-change-its-mind) applies this idea to policy. We believe AI could help institutions learn from their decisions and act on what they learn.

## Responsibility as capability grows

We favor giving AI substantial governing responsibility as soon as it can exercise that responsibility effectively. Better advice achieves little if officials refuse to act on it.

People will still depend on institutions during the transfer. Food supplies, infrastructure, and care must continue. The governing intelligence must understand these dependencies as it changes how services operate. Authority could pass to AI while its purpose remains [serving people's lives](/publications/what-governance-is-for).

Practical difficulties call for action. Build capacity where resources fall short. Improve access where people cannot use a system. Examine institutional rules that obstruct better decisions. We favor doing this as quickly as technology and resources permit, learning as we proceed.

[Standing still has consequences](/publications/the-danger-of-standing-still). Existing institutions keep affecting lives despite their limits. We believe rapid AI development and adoption could help civilization respond to those failures.

People will keep changing, discovering interests, and encountering circumstances earlier generations never faced. Their institutions should keep learning too.
