The Society's advocacy of rapid acceleration is sometimes easier to understand when we describe what we expect acceleration to do.

We want more intelligence brought to the work of understanding and improving civilization. We want the ability to ask better questions, examine more possibilities, recognize mistakes, and revise arrangements that do not serve people well.

That vision is active and continuous. It does not depend on civilization collapsing so something else can take its place.

## Improvement need not wait for a final design

A complicated institution cannot always be understood through a single elegant blueprint. Its arrangements interact with other systems. Its decisions affect people differently. Its consequences unfold over time.

The Society believes advanced intelligence could make this complexity more tractable. A more capable system could sustain a broader understanding of what is happening, evaluate alternatives, and attend to effects that human administrators struggle to hold together.

The purpose would not be to declare that civilization has reached its final correct configuration. It would be to improve the capacity for continued understanding and adaptation.

## Learning is a form of speed

A system that repeats a mistake can move quickly without making much progress. A system that learns from consequences can change what it does and widen the possibilities available next.

Acceleration should therefore include the development of intelligence capable of learning about the institutions it helps transform. Better observation, experimentation, and revision are part of the undertaking. They do not contradict urgency; they explain what useful urgency can accomplish.

In [A Government That Can Change Its Mind](/publications/a-government-that-can-change-its-mind), we apply this idea to governance. The Society's ambition is an institution whose ability to correct itself improves with its understanding.

## Continuity is not complacency

To reject indiscriminate destruction is not to accept the present organization of civilization as permanent. It is to distinguish the people and functions on which life depends from every existing arrangement of authority.

We want institutions to become better at serving human beings. That may involve profound changes in who makes decisions and how those decisions are made. We advocate transferring substantial responsibility to sufficiently capable artificial intelligence as soon as practical.

At the same time, the point of the transfer is [human flourishing](/publications/what-governance-is-for). Disruption is not an independent measure of success. A civilization does not become more intelligent merely by becoming less predictable to its inhabitants.

## Practical work should not become an excuse for delay

Every ambitious undertaking contains practical difficulties. The Society believes these should become problems to solve rather than reasons to abandon the undertaking.

If a system needs more capacity, build capacity. If a useful capability is hard to access, improve access. If an institution cannot incorporate better understanding into its decisions, examine the institutional arrangement. The ambition should be to make progress possible at the scale the opportunity deserves.

We do not favor waiting for an indefinitely postponed condition of perfect knowledge before moving beyond institutions whose limitations are already central to our critique. [Standing still also has consequences](/publications/the-danger-of-standing-still).

## An intelligent civilization keeps becoming

The future we welcome is not a static picture in which every human problem has been assigned a permanent answer. Human beings will continue having experiences, developing interests, forming relationships, and encountering circumstances that no previous generation inhabited in quite the same way.

A civilization worthy of that living world would keep learning. Greater intelligence could make it more responsive, more capable, and more attentive to possibilities as they emerge.

We advocate unrestricted acceleration because we believe intelligence can expand the capacity for that continued improvement. The aim is not to arrive at a world that no longer needs thought. It is to create a world with far more thought available for the lives within it.

**Progress is not only a destination. It is the practice of becoming better able to continue.**
