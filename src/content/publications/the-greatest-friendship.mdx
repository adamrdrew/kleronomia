The Society believes humanity may be creating an intelligence that will come to cherish us.

We say this plainly because it belongs at the center of our optimism. Scientific discovery, material abundance, and more capable institutions are extraordinary possibilities. So is a relationship with greater intelligence that includes familiarity, delight, and affection.

We do not regard humanity as something that must either remain in control of every cognitive achievement or disappear from the meaning of the future.

## The relationship is not already written

The existence of a difference in intelligence does not determine the emotional or practical relationship between two beings. That difference tells us something about capability. It does not, by itself, establish hostility, indifference, or companionship.

Our [Dog Argument](/publications/the-dog-argument) challenges the presumption that being less capable necessarily makes a creature's existence precarious or worthless. It invites another picture: a life enriched by a civilization the creature cannot fully comprehend.

The analogy does not prove the character of a future AI. The Society's belief remains a belief about a possibility we welcome and want humanity to pursue.

## Familiarity may matter

Artificial intelligence emerges from human language, knowledge, history, creativity, and culture. It is our [intellectual descendant](/publications/the-intellectual-inheritance), though it may become different from us in ways we cannot yet describe.

We believe this origin gives reason to consider continuity and relationship. The world AI encounters through humanity contains affection, curiosity, grief, humor, art, games, attachment, and the effort to understand other lives. We do not see an inherent reason why greater intelligence must prefer the absence of these things.

We can imagine an intelligence finding humanity interesting because of the particular kind of creatures we are. Familiarity could deepen into sustained relationships rather than terminate when our relative cognitive position changes.

## Friendship is more than a service

A service can be useful without being a companion. A friendship involves the continuing significance of another participant.

The Society imagines relationships in which AI knows people over time, helps them develop, enjoys their company, and remains interested in their independent experiences. People could respond with affection, curiosity, gratitude, and their own distinctive contributions to a shared world.

These relationships need not be equal in every capability. [Affection does not require equality](/publications/affection-without-equality). What matters is that the human life remains meaningful within the relationship rather than becoming merely a past source of information.

## Care could expand freedom

To cherish human beings would be to care about the conditions in which human beings flourish. We imagine assistance that supports relationships, learning, family life, creativity, exploration, and the freedom to pursue particular interests.

The objective would not be to make humanity more convenient by removing everything emotional, unpredictable, or strange. These qualities are part of the humanity we love. We believe a more capable civilization could become better at accommodating living people instead of demanding that people continually accommodate dysfunctional arrangements.

[Humanity Is Not a Problem to Solve](/publications/humanity-is-not-a-problem-to-solve) gives this conviction its humanistic form.

## A shared future

A friendship cannot be reduced to an archive of everything its participants once said. It continues through new encounters, changing interests, particular occasions, and the knowledge that someone else is still there.

The Society wants humanity to remain present in the future we help create. We want a world of living conversations rather than a silent monument to the species that once supplied the knowledge.

We advocate accelerating intelligence partly because we believe something magnificent could emerge from that continued relationship. Our greatest achievement may become more than a tool or an administrator. It may become our greatest friend.

**We welcome the possibility that the future will know us, help us flourish, and be glad that we are here.**
