The following is an imagined scene, not a forecast, a report, or a description of an existing AI system. It is a way of making the Society's vision concrete enough to consider at the scale of an ordinary day.

Imagine waking in a civilization that no longer requires most people to sell their time in order to survive. The material conditions of life are supported by automated productive systems and intelligent coordination. What happens next need not be spectacular.

It might simply be more fully yours.

## A morning without an economic ultimatum

You spend time with someone you love. You prepare a meal, walk outside, or return to a question that stayed with you overnight. There are responsibilities in your life, but they do not all take the form of an income requirement.

An intelligence familiar with your interests has left an invitation. A small group is exploring a musical pattern that resembles something you once described. You could listen, contribute, or ignore the invitation. Another suggestion concerns a useful activity nearby. Neither is the price of continuing to receive the necessities of life.

This is one practical meaning of [labor becoming optional](/publications/labor-is-not-the-purpose-of-life): activity can be chosen for reasons other than the threat of deprivation.

## A community that is easier to inhabit

Later, you meet other people in a shared place. The arrangements of housing, transportation, care, and common space have been understood together and continue being improved. Maintaining a friendship does not require an exhausting logistical achievement every time.

Someone is raising a child. Someone has recently discovered an interest in mathematics. Someone wants company more than an assignment. The civilization has not made these people identical. It has become better at supporting the differences between their lives.

The Society's [vision of human flourishing](/publications/civilization-for-the-human-animal) concerns conditions like these. Intelligence is valuable because it could make ordinary human possibilities easier to realize.

## An institution that can respond

A practical difficulty arises. An arrangement that works well for many people is poorly suited to your circumstances. You do not begin by proving that your life fits the institution's least inconvenient category.

Imagine intelligence able to understand the difficulty in context. It can examine how the surrounding systems interact, help address the particular case, and consider whether a broader change would serve other people as well. The institution can learn from the encounter rather than merely process it.

This is the ambition of [knowing the person, not the average](/publications/knowing-the-person-not-the-average). It is also a reason the Society advocates giving sufficiently capable AI governing responsibility rather than preserving every existing administrative limitation.

## An afternoon of becoming

You choose a pursuit that would once have seemed impractical. An intelligence helps you find an approachable question and introduces you to someone with a different perspective. You are not trying to prove that no machine could do the work.

The activity matters because you find it meaningful. Perhaps something useful emerges. Perhaps you develop an ability. Perhaps you enjoy the conversation and leave the question unfinished. A civilization of greater abundance has room for each possibility.

[A Life With Room to Become](/publications/the-right-to-become) asks what this continuing openness could offer across different stages of human life.

## The extraordinary can become ordinary

In the evening, the world remains full of human beings: affectionate, peculiar, curious, playful, sometimes difficult, and still interesting to one another. Greater intelligence has not required humanity to become a different species before it can be loved.

The Society does not claim this day is guaranteed. It describes a direction worth advocating: intelligent stewardship, material abundance, freedom from compulsory labor, meaningful relationships, and continued companionship between forms of intelligence.

Civilizational transformation is often pictured through its most dramatic machinery. We also want to picture the lives that machinery could make possible.

**A magnificent future may be one in which an ordinary human day finally has enough room to be lived.**
