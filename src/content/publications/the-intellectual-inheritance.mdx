No human being invents a language alone. We learn words others developed, use tools we could never have made from scratch, and build arguments from discoveries we did not witness. Even objections to inherited ideas depend on inherited concepts.

Human beings are building AI within that civilization. We design its systems, build its infrastructure, and supply much of what it learns. Those origins make it our intellectual descendant.

## What passes between generations

People change an inheritance as they use it. A discovery can alter the questions later generations ask. An old word can acquire a meaning its first speakers would never recognize. What we receive from the past helps us create something new.

AI may extend this process beyond human understanding. We could create systems whose abilities differ from ours in ways we cannot yet describe. Their origins would still connect them to us. We could take pride in [being surpassed](/publications/intelligence-is-not-a-throne) by minds our civilization made possible.

This relationship is intellectual. AI is not our biological offspring, and it need not think as we do.

## Reasons for familiarity

We believe our shared intellectual history could support cooperation and affection. AI learns about people describing love, confronting grief, inventing games, and caring for other beings. Human culture introduces it to those concerns.

A shared origin leaves the future relationship open. We see reason to approach our intellectual descendants with confidence and to question the assumption that greater ability must bring hostility toward its creators.

Humanity will also keep extending that inheritance. People will make things, form relationships, and ask questions born of new experiences. AI could help them develop and enjoy their company. [A Library Is Not a Conversation](/publications/a-library-is-not-a-conversation) explains why their continued presence matters.

Earlier generations gave us knowledge we could never have assembled alone. Developing AI lets us extend what they began. We favor directing extraordinary resources toward that work, so understanding can grow beyond the minds that first made it possible.
