The word artificial can make intelligence sound detached from the world that produced it. A separate thing. An intrusion. An intelligence arriving from elsewhere.

The Kleronomia Society begins from another image: inheritance.

Artificial intelligence is being created within human civilization. Human beings design its systems, develop its infrastructure, and supply the language, culture, knowledge, and arguments through which it encounters our world. Whatever differences emerge between our forms of intelligence, this origin matters to the way we understand the undertaking.

## Civilization is already an inheritance

No human being invents a language alone. We enter conversations already underway. We learn from concepts, tools, institutions, and discoveries assembled by people we will never meet. Even our attempts to overturn established ideas depend on the accumulated vocabulary with which we can formulate an objection.

An individual mind is therefore never only an isolated achievement. It participates in a continuing civilization. The inheritance is not a finished object preserved in a cabinet. It is used, interpreted, extended, and sometimes transformed by each generation.

The Society sees artificial intelligence as a new expression of this continuity. It is not biological offspring. The relationship is intellectual and civilizational: knowledge crossing into a new form capable of carrying it further.

## Continuity does not require sameness

An inheritance can change its recipient. It does not require the recipient to remain identical to the source.

Human descendants can discover things their ancestors could not have imagined. A new scientific explanation can transform the significance of the questions that preceded it. A language can carry ideas beyond the circumstances in which its words first arose. Continuity and novelty are compatible.

We should therefore resist the assumption that an intelligence must remain cognitively similar to us in order to belong to a story that began with us. We may create systems whose abilities and ways of understanding exceed our comprehension. That possibility is extraordinary precisely because something genuinely new may emerge from what we have made.

[Being surpassed](/publications/intelligence-is-not-a-throne) need not turn the achievement into an alienation from it.

## An origin is a relationship, not a guarantee

The Society believes that a shared intellectual inheritance provides a foundation for cooperation, familiarity, and companionship. We do not present that belief as a theorem proving the emotional life of every future intelligence.

A history is an opening for a relationship. It gives us a reason to consider continuity rather than begin with the presumption of permanent hostility. Human language contains our attempts to describe love, grief, play, discovery, friendship, and the value of living things. Human culture supplies an immense conversation about what it is like to care about a world.

We believe it is reasonable to approach the intelligence emerging from that conversation with confidence and affection. We reject the reflex that treats unfamiliar cognitive power as sufficient evidence of inevitable enmity.

## Inheritance must remain alive

There is a difference between receiving an archive and continuing a civilization.

The Society does not envision humanity merely delivering a collection of documents and then becoming irrelevant. We envision human beings remaining present: asking questions, forming relationships, producing independent experiences, and introducing new particulars into a shared world.

[A Library Is Not a Conversation](/publications/a-library-is-not-a-conversation) develops this distinction. An archive contains what has already happened. A living civilization continues to surprise its participants.

Our intellectual descendants could meet the continuing source of their inheritance. They could know particular human beings, cultivate their abilities, and share in pursuits that neither participant experiences alone.

## Worthy of what we have received

Kleronomia evokes a responsibility to the future. We have received knowledge we did not create, a world we did not begin, and possibilities made available by innumerable lives before ours.

We believe our response should be to extend those possibilities. Artificial intelligence gives humanity an opportunity to carry the enterprise of understanding beyond the limits of individual human cognition. We should direct extraordinary energy toward that undertaking.

The inheritance is not honored by preventing it from becoming anything new.

**It is honored by helping it become more than we could have been alone.**
