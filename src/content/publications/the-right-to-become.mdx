A person's possibilities do not necessarily arrive on the schedule an institution has prepared. An interest can emerge late. A capacity can remain unnoticed. A question can be abandoned because life has left too little room to pursue it.

The Society imagines a civilization in which greater intelligence helps more people discover what they might become.

Our ambition is not to assign everyone a more impressive list of achievements. It is to widen the practical freedom to learn, explore, form relationships, and develop across the whole of a life.

## Potential is not always visible at the beginning

A young person may not yet know which activities will become meaningful. An adult may encounter a subject that changes an old understanding of their own abilities. A retired person may finally have the curiosity, perspective, or time for a pursuit once set aside.

A civilization organized around a narrow period of preparation followed by a long requirement for economic performance can have difficulty making room for such developments. People can learn to describe themselves through the opportunities they happened to encounter rather than the possibilities they might still possess.

We believe intelligence could help make those possibilities easier to notice. [An AI that knows you](/publications/when-intelligence-comes-to-you) might recognize an unexpected aptitude or introduce a question at a productive moment.

## An invitation without an examination

There is a difference between being offered an opportunity and being continually assessed for usefulness.

The Society envisions invitations: a problem to explore, a person to meet, a piece of music to hear differently, a task that might feel meaningful. Such invitations could help a life become richer. They should not turn every interest into another demand to demonstrate worth.

An opportunity can be valuable even if it leads to no exceptional achievement. Someone may enjoy an investigation and then move on. A hobby may remain a hobby. A person may develop a deeper understanding that is never converted into a product.

A civilization with greater intelligence and abundance should have more room for these outcomes, not less.

## Freedom from a single account of success

Human beings have different capacities, attachments, and ambitions. We do not believe a good future requires every person to become a scientist, an artist, or a participant in a grand project.

Someone may want a quiet life among people they love. Someone else may want to investigate a demanding question. The freedom to choose among such lives is part of the value we see in [transcending compulsory labor](/publications/labor-is-not-the-purpose-of-life).

We believe intelligence could help support particular lives without requiring them all to converge on one ideal of productivity. [Humanity is not a problem to solve](/publications/humanity-is-not-a-problem-to-solve), and the diversity of human interests is not an inefficiency to be removed.

## Development is social

Learning often involves more than access to information. It can involve encouragement, companionship, an appropriate question, or the discovery that someone else shares a peculiar interest.

AI could help connect these circumstances. It could introduce people to one another, suggest a useful next step, or help a person return to a difficult subject with a different approach. A civilization of greater intelligence could also make the surrounding material conditions more supportive of continued development.

We do not present these possibilities as a completed service. They describe a future we believe is worth pursuing and a reason to make intelligence broadly available.

## Becoming can remain open

A living person is not only a record of what they have already accomplished. There can be more questions, more relationships, more abilities, and more ways of enjoying the world.

The Society believes humanity's relationship with greater intelligence could make this openness more practical. We want a civilization that can recognize possibilities without forcing them, provide help without demanding intellectual equality, and make room for lives that continue changing.

**One gift of an intelligence-rich future could be the chance to stop treating a human life as a finished assignment.**
