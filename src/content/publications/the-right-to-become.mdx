An interest can appear years after school ends. An adult can discover an ability they never had occasion to try. A retired person can return to a question work and family once left little time to pursue.

We imagine AI helping people recognize these possibilities throughout life. [An intelligence familiar with your interests](/publications/when-intelligence-comes-to-you) could notice a connection you missed or suggest a way into a difficult subject.

A pursuit can matter without producing an exceptional achievement. Someone may enjoy an instrument without performing in public. A hobby can occupy years without becoming a business. An investigation can remain unfinished and still repay the time spent on it.

We want civilization to support these choices. Abundance could reduce the pressure to turn every interest into income or evidence of usefulness. Invitations from AI should leave people free to refuse. A person's potential gives no one authority to assign them tasks or keep assessing their worth.

Some people want demanding projects. Others want quiet lives with people they love. Interests change, and periods of care or rest can matter as much as periods of achievement. [Freedom from compulsory labor](/publications/labor-is-not-the-purpose-of-life) would make these choices practical for more people. Our account of [human value](/publications/humanity-is-not-a-problem-to-solve) makes room for preferences no institution would prescribe.

Learning also needs encouragement and company. AI could introduce people who share an unusual interest, suggest a next question, or offer a different approach to an old difficulty. More capable institutions could improve the conditions that let people keep learning.

We want to develop these abilities and make them widely available. Help that responds to a person's circumstances could let them keep changing throughout life. Past achievements would leave open what they might still enjoy becoming.
