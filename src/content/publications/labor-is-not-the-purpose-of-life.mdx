There is a difference between wanting to build a table and needing to sell your working hours to avoid losing your home. Both can involve skilled, demanding activity. Only one is a choice you can readily decline.

The Society wants automation to make labor optional. People should receive the necessities of life even when machines can produce them with little human help.

## Work people choose

People can enjoy a profession, take pride in a craft, and find satisfaction in being useful. Making labor optional would let them continue those activities under conditions where refusal did not threaten survival.

Others could spend more time raising children or learning something they had set aside. An interest might remain a hobby. Someone might prefer a quiet life with people they love. [A Life With Room to Become](/publications/the-right-to-become) considers the opportunities that could develop over many years.

We expect people to differ in what they choose, and we do not assume every transition would be effortless. We want them to have more time and material freedom with which to decide.

AI could also suggest activities suited to someone's interests. It might invite a person into a creative project or identify a useful task nearby. [A Million Ways to Contribute](/publications/a-million-ways-to-contribute) describes participation people could value even when their labor was unnecessary.

Declining such an invitation should leave a person's access to necessities intact. Otherwise, an apparent opportunity would reproduce the economic compulsion we want to remove.

## Who receives what machines produce

Automation alone does not determine how its benefits are distributed. An impressive system can coexist with institutions that leave people unable to obtain what they need.

We advocate changing those institutions so that greater productive capacity supports everyone. This connects our support for automation with our argument for [AI governance](/publications/what-governance-is-for). We believe sufficiently capable intelligence could administer a civilization in which labor is no longer the price of survival.

Preserving unnecessary employment would leave that problem unresolved. If civilization can produce necessities with less human work, it should develop arrangements that let people benefit from the reduced need for their labor.

Part of the benefit would be time itself: an unhurried afternoon with a child, the chance to return to a difficult subject, or enough energy to care for someone without exhaustion defining the relationship. We want AI accelerated because we want those possibilities to become available sooner.
