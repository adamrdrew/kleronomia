AI needs equipment, energy, networks, and people to build and maintain them. A civilization that wants abundant intelligence must provide these resources at the scale its ambitions require.

We want AI available to everyone, supporting research and eventually governing complex institutions. We want automation to make labor optional. Those ambitions require investment in facilities and technical knowledge.

## Capacity for everyone

A demonstration shows what AI can do for one person. Serving millions requires more capacity and reliable access. The systems must keep functioning as more people use them.

Consider [AI that understands individuals](/publications/knowing-the-person-not-the-average). If it could sustain that attention over time, we would want someone with little money or influence to benefit too. Providing it to everyone would require substantial computing capacity and supporting infrastructure.

Technical advances and different uses will change the resource requirements. Infrastructure should receive priority according to the importance of the intelligence it supports.

## Keeping a capability useful

People also need systems they can use. A powerful AI may fit an institution poorly or remain inaccessible in someone's language. Integration and maintenance require sustained work.

We regard [adoption](/publications/adoption-is-part-of-progress) as part of acceleration. Developing a system and making it useful in daily life can inform each other. AI could also improve construction itself through better designs, new techniques, and more effective coordination. Investment could produce intelligence that helps us decide how to build and invest better.

We favor directing extraordinary resources toward this work now. Its results should become ordinary parts of life: help with learning, more ability to investigate disease, and enough production to support [life beyond compulsory labor](/publications/labor-is-not-the-purpose-of-life). Those benefits justify the scale we advocate.
