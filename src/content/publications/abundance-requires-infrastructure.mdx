A future of abundant intelligence will not be constructed from declarations alone. It will need the material and intellectual means through which intelligence can be developed and made available.

The Society's advocacy of acceleration therefore includes an unromantic conviction: build the infrastructure.

Our ambitions extend to scientific discovery, universal access, the automation of labor, and the intelligent coordination of civilization. Those ambitions imply substantial practical work. The scale of the opportunity should be reflected in the resources directed toward it.

## Intelligence has material conditions

Artificial intelligence is not an exception to the fact that technology requires a world in which it can operate. Computing equipment, energy, networks, facilities, technical knowledge, and skilled work are part of the undertaking.

A civilization that wants greater capabilities must create the conditions for those capabilities to exist. Hoping for abundance while refusing the work required to produce it confuses the destination with the journey.

We do not attach an invented number to the resources the future will require. Different systems, technical advances, and forms of deployment will create different requirements. Our claim is about priority: the infrastructure that makes the growth of intelligence possible should be treated as a central civilizational investment.

## Capacity creates room for possibility

An intelligence-rich civilization would need to do more than answer occasional questions for a small audience. The Society imagines systems capable of supporting research, understanding complex institutions, and sustaining useful relationships with people at enormous scale.

If an AI can [know an individual well](/publications/knowing-the-person-not-the-average), the significance of that capacity changes when it can be extended beyond the fortunate few. If intelligent tools can help people learn or create, abundance means making those tools broadly available rather than preserving them as scarce privileges.

Infrastructure is part of the difference between a remarkable demonstration and a widely shared transformation. Its social meaning lies in the possibilities it can make ordinary.

## Build for use, not only for spectacle

A civilization is not transformed merely because a system produces an impressive result. Capabilities must meet the circumstances in which human beings actually live and institutions actually operate.

That requires the less conspicuous work of integration, access, maintenance, and continued improvement. The Society sees this work as part of acceleration. It is not a lesser activity that begins after the important invention has already happened.

[Adoption is part of progress](/publications/adoption-is-part-of-progress). A capability that remains difficult to use, unavailable to most people, or disconnected from consequential decisions has not yet realized the full promise we see in it.

## An ambition proportionate to the achievement

Humanity regularly directs enormous collective effort toward the arrangements it considers necessary. The Society believes the development of intelligence should stand among our highest priorities.

Greater intelligence could help us understand how to build the future more effectively. It could improve designs, discover new techniques, and help coordinate complex projects. These are possibilities for the development process itself to become more capable as the work proceeds.

We therefore envision investment and intelligence reinforcing one another. Resources support better systems; better systems can help us understand how to use resources more effectively. This is an argument for beginning and continuing the work with urgency.

## Abundance should arrive in people's lives

The aim is not a world that admires its infrastructure while human beings remain constrained by the same narrow possibilities. The aim is what the infrastructure enables.

More room to investigate a disease. More help for a person trying to learn. More productive capacity that can make [compulsory labor unnecessary](/publications/labor-is-not-the-purpose-of-life). More ability to understand the institutions on which people depend.

The Society advocates the rapid development of the material foundations for that future. Our optimism is a reason to build, and to build with an ambition equal to the importance of intelligence.

**Abundance is not a mood. It is a possibility we should give ourselves the means to realize.**
