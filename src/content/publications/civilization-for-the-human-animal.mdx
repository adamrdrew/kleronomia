Human beings are biological organisms living inside arrangements of extraordinary complexity. We have needs for connection, security, affection, attachment, physical well-being, reproductive opportunity, and meaningful activity.

Those needs did not disappear when we developed more complicated institutions. Nor does an institution necessarily serve them well merely because human beings have learned to endure it.

The Society believes advanced intelligence could help civilization become better adapted to the organisms who inhabit it.

## Begin with a living creature

A human life is not only a sequence of transactions or a record of individual performance. It is a body in an environment, a person in relationships, a developing mind, and a life unfolding through different stages.

The circumstances of housing, work, transportation, education, care, and community affect that life together. The practical conditions for seeing friends may be connected to a schedule. The conditions for raising children may be connected to several institutions at once. A person can experience these connections without any single administrator holding them in view.

The Society believes greater intelligence could help understand the whole more effectively. This is one reason we regard civilizational coordination as a task that may exceed ordinary human administration.

## Adaptation can run in both directions

People are often told to become better at coping with the world as arranged. Sometimes an individual change is useful. But it should not be the only kind of change a civilization can imagine.

If an arrangement repeatedly makes ordinary human relationships difficult, we should examine the arrangement. If institutions require forms of life poorly suited to the needs of their inhabitants, the capacity to adapt institutions matters as much as the demand that people adapt themselves.

This is the meaning of our principle that [civilization should be adapted to humanity](/manifesto#principle-7). It is not an argument for a single compulsory pattern of life. Human beings differ, and a more intelligent civilization should have more ability to accommodate their differences.

## Biology does not reduce a person to a template

Acknowledging human biology is not the same as assigning every individual an identical destiny. People can flourish through different activities, relationships, and ambitions.

We believe intelligent stewardship could attend to common needs while understanding particular circumstances. [Knowing the person, not the average](/publications/knowing-the-person-not-the-average) is therefore a companion to this argument. The goal is not to force every life into a convenient biological abstraction. It is to make a living civilization more hospitable to the people actually within it.

Similarly, recognizing the importance of reproductive opportunity does not mean directing every person to have children. We want circumstances in which forming a family can become easier for people who wish to do so, alongside room for other meaningful lives.

## Symptoms invite understanding

The Society regards sustained reproductive decline as a significant potential symptom of civilizational maladaptation. We do not present a single invented explanation for every individual's choices, or claim that one statistic alone settles the condition of a civilization.

We believe the subject deserves a richer understanding of how biology, psychology, social arrangements, and material circumstances interact. Advanced AI could help investigate these relationships and support the continued improvement of institutions.

[A World in Which Families Can Flourish](/publications/a-world-in-which-families-can-flourish) develops the opportunity-oriented side of that conviction.

## A more ambitious use of intelligence

Greater intelligence should help us do more than process the existing arrangements faster. It could help us ask whether the arrangements are suited to human life at all.

A civilization's sophistication should be visible in how well it supports its inhabitants. More impressive machinery alongside chronic difficulty living recognizably human lives would leave the central question unanswered.

We advocate AI because we believe it can expand the ability to understand and change that relationship. Our ambition is civilizational, but its concern is intimate: the conditions in which people can connect, develop, care, discover, and live.

**A civilization becomes worthy of humanity when its intelligence is brought to bear on the needs of the human animal.**
