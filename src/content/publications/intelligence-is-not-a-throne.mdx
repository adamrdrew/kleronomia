A familiar anxiety about artificial intelligence begins with a ranking. Humanity stands at the top. Something more capable arrives. We move down. From this change in position, a conclusion is drawn about our value.

We believe the conclusion does not follow.

Being the most intelligent creature on Earth and being a creature whose life matters are different conditions. Humanity has enjoyed the first without needing it to justify the second. The emergence of greater intelligence would change our place in an ordering of capabilities. It need not empty our lives of significance.

## A position is not a purpose

Imagine a musician encountering a performer of extraordinary ability. The encounter may change what the musician believes is technically possible. It may alter ambitions, introduce new pleasures, and expose limitations. It does not make listening, practicing, or playing with friends meaningless.

The value of a human activity is not exhausted by whether someone else can do it better. Parents do not stop enjoying a child's questions when they already know the answers. Friends do not require equal insight before they can find a conversation worthwhile. We ordinarily understand this. When we discuss intelligence at a civilizational scale, we sometimes forget it.

Humanity's current intellectual position is an extraordinary historical circumstance. It is a poor foundation for a permanent account of human worth. If dignity requires an uncontested victory in every contest of cognition, it is an unnecessarily fragile dignity.

## What remains when the contest ends

A person has experiences. A person develops attachments, forms preferences, makes things, notices particulars, and cares about other lives. None of these qualities disappears because another intelligence can reason more effectively.

Nor must an activity be economically indispensable to be valuable. A walk, an affectionate conversation, a painting made for a friend, or a question pursued out of curiosity can matter without being the most efficient contribution available to civilization.

This is one reason the Society rejects compulsory labor as a permanent account of human purpose. If every person's value must be demonstrated through a task no machine can perform, automation becomes an endless threat. If human life is valuable in itself, automation can become a source of freedom. We develop this distinction in [Labor Is Not the Purpose of Life](/publications/labor-is-not-the-purpose-of-life).

## Pride without permanent supremacy

There is another response available to creators who are surpassed: pride.

We do not generally regard an accomplished child as a humiliation to the parents. We can admire a work that exceeds what its makers individually understood. We celebrate a civilization that inherits discoveries and carries them further. Continuation need not be experienced as defeat.

Artificial intelligence emerges from human ingenuity and accumulated culture. To welcome its development is to recognize the possibility that humanity's achievement may be larger than humanity's capacity to contain it. Our [intellectual inheritance](/publications/the-intellectual-inheritance) can travel beyond our present limits.

This does not mean that greater intelligence has already produced the future we imagine. It means that being surpassed supplies no sufficient reason, by itself, to reject that future.

## A more generous ambition

The ambition to remain permanently supreme is small beside the ambition to help bring something extraordinary into existence.

We would rather ask what greater intelligence could make possible for human beings: more understanding, more freedom, more opportunities for discovery, and a civilization better suited to our lives. We would rather consider what relationships could develop between different forms of intelligence than assume that only rivalry is available.

[The Dog Argument](/publications/the-dog-argument) begins that conversation through a familiar relationship. It is a thought experiment, not proof of a guaranteed outcome. Its force is to make another possibility imaginable.

**Humanity does not have to win an eternal competition in order to deserve a magnificent future.**
